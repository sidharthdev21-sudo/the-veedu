## 2. Complete voiceover (first person)

Voice: warm, unhurried, thoughtful; a person explaining their own work, not an advert. "…" marks a pause. The TTS script in `build/vo.py` uses phonetic respellings for Kerala place names. The text below uses the correct spellings.

**1. Opening hook.** Not everyone who travels to Kochi is on holiday. … Some come because someone they love has been admitted to hospital. And for the rest of the family, one question arrives quickly: where do we stay? … That's the moment I wanted to understand.

**2. The product idea.** I'm building The Veedu, an accommodation discovery platform for Kerala. My background is in UX design and running rental accommodation, and I already had a working prototype. … But a prototype isn't evidence. So before building any further, I set out to find whether it deserved to exist.

**3. Customer problem hypothesis.** My starting hypothesis was simple. Medical travellers and their families need things general booking platforms don't surface well: being close to the hospital, staying for weeks, and knowing the real cost. … I wrote down what would prove that wrong before I looked for anything that proved it right.

**4. Market and tourism context.** The official data gave me context — and a warning. Kerala recorded around two and a half crore domestic tourist visits in 2025, and in 2023, Ernakulam, Kochi's district, led the state. About six point four lakh foreigners came to India for medical treatment in 2024. … But tourist visits aren't medical travellers. And the Ministry of Tourism doesn't keep state-wise medical tourism data. The number I needed most didn't exist.

**5. Competitor research.** So I went to the ground: Amrita Hospital and Aster Medcity, in the Edappally–Cheranalloor corridor. At Amrita, one family member can stay in the patient's room, and other relatives are pointed to a guest house. I mapped twelve providers nearby, from budget rooms to a Marriott, and some already market to patients and bystanders. … So this wasn't an empty market. The question changed from whether there's supply, to what's missing.

**6. Customer sentiment insights.** The reviews were where it got interesting. I could only read twenty-five excerpts, well short of the hundred I wanted, so I treat them as a signal, not a statistic. Guests praised staff and cleanliness. They complained about extra charges at checkout, and maintenance on longer stays. … And when reviewers mentioned the hospital, they wrote about distance. One guest house named after Amrita was a twelve-minute walk to one guest — and a little away to another.

**7. The insight and market sizing.** That became the insight everything else builds on. Distance is why families choose a stay, and it's the fact listings state least consistently. … To size the market, I started with something I could count: over two thousand beds across the two hospitals. Every step after that is an explicit assumption. The base case projects around sixteen crore rupees a year in accommodation spend, with about eight crore a platform could realistically serve.

**8. Business feasibility.** Then the harder question: could a small pilot work? With ten partner properties and a twelve percent commission, the base case projects about seven lakh in platform revenue, and falls short of break-even, which needs around fifty-five bookings a month. … Short, cheap stays lose money on every booking. The model only works for longer stays — the same families the research pointed to. So the pilot should be small, founder-led, and built to measure, not to scale.

**9. Product strategy.** From there, the strategy narrowed. The primary customer is an out-of-town family caring for a patient admitted at Amrita or Aster Medcity. Their problem isn't finding a room. It's trusting the facts behind it. … So the position I want to earn is simple: know how far, how much, and for how long, before you call.

**10. MVP prioritisation.** I scored nine features on reach, impact, confidence and effort. The MVP keeps five: hospital-based discovery, verified distance to the entrance, all-in pricing for the whole family, dated verification, and tracked WhatsApp enquiries. … Online booking and payment can wait. And because my own prototype still shows straight-line distance, verification is the first thing to build.

**11. Validation roadmap.** None of this is validated yet, so the next stage is a set of small experiments. Interview caregivers near both hospitals. Talk to hospital coordinators about how families are referred. Verify ten properties in person, and measure what it costs. … Then run a few weeks of WhatsApp-led matching, tracking how many enquiries become confirmed stays, and how many families go direct the next time.

**12. Closing reflection.** I haven't proven a business. What I've done is narrow an idea I care about into a specific customer, a specific problem, and a test small enough to run. … For me, that's the job. Not to be right at the start — but to find out, honestly and as cheaply as possible, what deserves to be built.

## 4. Music and sound design
- **Score:** a minimal ambient pad (four-chord loop in D major at about 70 BPM) with a soft bell on chord changes. It's synthesised in `build/build.py`, so there are no licensing issues. To use a licensed track, pick felt piano and pad, no percussion, major key.
- **Levels:** VO at about −16 LUFS integrated; music about 5 dB under speech with automatic ducking; mix normalised to −16 LUFS (−1.5 dBTP).
- **Moments:**
  - a low sustained tone under "the number I needed most didn't exist"
  - about 1.2 s of near-silence after "a little away", letting the strongest finding land
  - the pad resolves under the closing line
  - dawn ambience under the end card
- **UI sounds:** soft low ticks on card and chip reveals; airy whooshes (about −30 dB) on four chapter turns. Avoid risers, impacts and hospital sounds.

## 5. Editing and transitions
- **Rhythm:** one idea per scene. The voice carries the explanation and the screen holds at most one headline and three supporting items.
- **Cuts:** 0.5 s cross-fades between scenes, with whooshes only at chapter turns (context, insight, strategy, roadmap). No spins, glitches or flashes.
- **Motion:** elements rise 20 px with an ease-out over 0.7 s, timed to the sentence that names them (sentence timestamps come from `build/vo.py`). Charts draw rather than pop.
- **Colour rules:**
  - cyan for primary figures and attention
  - violet for hypotheses, caveats and comparisons
  - lime only for the key insight (distance), the MVP decision and the "build first" call
  - green only for the recommended pilot
- **Integrity on screen:**
  - every figure carries a source line or a "Projected" tag
  - the hypothesis, sentiment sample and roadmap carry "not yet tested", "signal, not statistic" and "proposed · not yet run" tags
  - no UI is shown as a live product
- **Subtitles:** `build/the-veedu-case-study.srt` is embedded as a soft track. Burn it in for social platforms that autoplay muted.

## 7. Short portfolio description

> **The Veedu: a Product Management case study (4:49 film).** I took my own prototype, an accommodation platform for families travelling to Kochi for hospital treatment, and asked whether it deserved to exist. Using official tourism data, on-the-ground competitor mapping around two hospitals, review analysis and a bottom-up market model, I found the strongest insight: distance is why families choose a stay, and it's the fact listings state least consistently. That shaped a narrow strategy, a five-feature MVP built around verified information, and an eight-week validation plan. Nothing is claimed as proven; the film shows how I decide what to build and how I'd find out if I'm wrong.
