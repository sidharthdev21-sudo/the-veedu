### Sentiment analysis methodology

1. **Collect:** gather reviews and feedback from the sources above for comparable women's ethnic sets and kurta sets.
2. **Clean:** remove duplicates, spam, irrelevant and incomplete comments, and suspicious template reviews (for example, unfilled placeholders such as "(mention the fabric)").
3. **Classify:** label each item positive, negative, neutral or mixed.
4. **Tag themes:** comfort, fabric quality, fit and sizing, design, price, durability, transparency, colour accuracy, delivery, returns and exchanges.
5. **Generate insights:** what customers appreciate, complain about, expect and wish existed, and what competitors may be failing to address.

## Market sizing: formulas and assumptions

### TAM: total addressable market (top-down)

**TAM = Sarees + Sets** (women's Indian wear, all channels, all prices, all ages)
= ₹564 bn + ₹415 bn = **₹979 bn in FY2024** (≈ ₹97,900 crore) → ₹1,532 bn by FY2029.

- *Assumption:* Aalayam's co-ord sets fall within Technopak's "Sets". The report doesn't define co-ords separately.
- The whole women's apparel market (₹2,298 bn) is **not** used as TAM, because most of it is categories Aalayam doesn't sell.
- Lehenga and mix & match are excluded. Mix & match (₹297 bn) is a possible future extension.
- No double counting: sarees and sets are separate lines in the report.

### SAM: serviceable available market (Aalayam's segment, all of India, online)

**SAM = TAM × online share × mid-price share × share from women aged 20–40**

| Step | Filter | Value | Result (FY2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| TAM | — | — | ₹979 bn |
| 1 | Online (e-commerce share of women's apparel) | 20% | ₹195.8 bn |
| 2 | Mid-price segment (Aalayam at ₹1,000–1,700) | 27% | ₹52.9 bn |
| 3 | Women aged 20–40 | ~44% | **₹23.3 bn (≈ ₹2,330 crore)** |

- **Filter 1 assumption:** sarees and sets sell online at the same rate as women's apparel overall. Sarees may sell online less, so this may overstate SAM.
- **Filter 2 assumption:** women's Indian wear has the same price mix as all apparel. Aalayam's ₹1,000–1,700 sits around the ₹1,200 mid-price kurta benchmark, and below the ₹3,700 mid-price saree benchmark, so its sarees may count as value-to-mid.
- **Filter 3 is a hypothesis.** Women aged roughly 20–40 are about 33% of the population: half of 15–24 (≈9%), plus 25–34 (17%), plus half of 35–44 (≈7%). That is about 44% of people aged 15 and over. Using it assumes spending is proportional to population. Young adults may spend more on fashion, so a survey should check this.
- **Projection:** with FY2029 inputs (TAM ₹1,532 bn, 25% online, 27.5% mid-price, 44%), SAM ≈ **₹46.3 bn (≈ ₹4,630 crore)**.

### SOM: serviceable obtainable market (bottom-up, three-year plan)

**Revenue = reachable target customers × visit rate × purchase conversion × orders per buyer per year × average order value (AOV).** Returning customers are added on top.

**All inputs below are labelled planning assumptions, not Aalayam's data.** Replace them with real figures from Instagram, Shopify and ads.

| Input | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Women reached (unique, paid + organic) | 500,000 | 1,200,000 | 2,500,000 | Assumption: ramping ad budget and content |
| Reach → store visit | 6% | 6% | 6% | Assumption |
| Visit → first purchase | 1.5% | 1.5% | 1.5% | Assumption; no reliable Indian fashion benchmark found |
| **New buyers** | **450** | **1,080** | **2,250** | Calculated |
| Returning buyers (25% of all earlier buyers each year) | 0 | 112 | 382 | Assumption; Indian D2C 90-day repeat is often cited at ~20–30% |
| **Active buyers** | **450** | **1,192** | **2,632** | Calculated (customer count) |
| Orders per active buyer per year | 1.3 | 1.3 | 1.3 | Assumption |
| Average order value | ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 | Brief: ₹1,000–1,700 per piece |
| **Gross revenue** | **₹8.8 lakh** | **₹23.2 lakh** | **₹51.3 lakh** | Calculated |
| Net of returns (25% assumed) | ₹6.6 lakh | ₹17.4 lakh | ₹38.5 lakh | Apparel returns are often cited at 20–35% |
| Orders per month (capacity check) | ~49 | ~129 | ~285 | About 10 a day by Year 3: manageable for a small team, but inventory must keep up |

### Top-down vs bottom-up

- Year 3 bottom-up SOM ≈ ₹0.51 crore gross, which is **about 0.02% of today's SAM** (₹2,330 crore).
- An arbitrary "1% of SAM" would give **₹23 crore**, roughly 45 times more than this plan's reach, conversion and capacity can support. That's why SOM is built bottom-up.
- **How sensitive it is:** conversion and reach drive the result. Doubling conversion to 3% roughly doubles revenue (≈ ₹1 crore in Year 3).
- **Missing inputs to replace:** actual reach (Instagram followers and ad reach), site traffic, conversion, AOV, repeat rate and return rate.

## Business opportunities and recommendations (to test, not current capabilities)

- **Priority segment:** women aged 20–40 buying online, in the mid-price band.
- **Focus categories:** comfortable everyday sets (including co-ords) and sarees.
- **Product improvements:** state fabric, lining and opacity; give garment measurements and fit notes; use true-to-colour photography.
- **Differentiation:** comfort you can verify, plus honest value (see Episode 3).
- **Growth planning:** replace the SOM assumptions with real reach, conversion, order value, repeat and return data, and re-run the model each quarter.
- **Test first:** size guidance and true-to-life photos, because the fit and "looks different online" complaints are the most frequent and cheapest to address.
