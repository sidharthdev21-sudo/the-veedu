# Five Forces: findings and business implications · Aalayam by Anamika

Part 2 of the Business Research Explainer Series.

**How to read this.** The observations are general and structural. No competitor rankings, market shares, prices, supplier names or statistics are presented, because verified figures were not part of this material. The actions are **potential recommendations**, not current capabilities.

| Force | What it means | What we investigated | Where we looked | What we observed | Why it matters | Actions to consider |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Competitive rivalry** | How intense the competition is | Competing brands, pricing, product categories, differentiation, reviews and ratings | Myntra & AJIO, Amazon India, Instagram *(used)*; competitor products *(observed directly)* | Shoppers see many similar outfits side by side | Standing out is harder, and price alone is easy to copy | Recognisable brand identity; comfort and design; better customer experience; don't compete on price alone |
| **Threat of new entrants** | How easily new brands can start | Launching online, e-commerce platforms, startup infrastructure, digital marketing, marketplace selling | Shopify & marketplaces, Instagram *(used)* | Starting an online clothing store is relatively easy | New competitors can appear quickly | Brand recognition; customer loyalty; consistent product identity; go beyond basic products |
| **Bargaining power of suppliers** | How much control suppliers have | Fabric suppliers, alternative manufacturers, fabric costs, minimum order quantities, reliability and delivery | Supplier quotes and discussions *(conducted)*; IndiaMART *(suggested next)* | Relying on a single supplier is a risk | Costs, quality and delivery depend on suppliers | More than one supplier; compare sourcing costs; keep quality standards; less single-supplier dependence |
| **Bargaining power of buyers** | How much power customers have | Price sensitivity, competing products, reviews, shopping preferences, brand switching | Amazon, Myntra & AJIO, Google Trends *(used)*; customer surveys and interviews *(conducted)* | With so many choices, customers can compare and switch easily | Customers have real power over price and loyalty | Better product quality; transparent pricing; build customer trust; encourage repeat purchases |
| **Threat of substitutes** | Other ways to meet the same need | Other clothing categories, local boutiques, offline retailers, ethnic and western wear, custom stitching | Marketplace listings, competitor sites *(used)*; customer interviews *(conducted)* | Customers always have other options | Aalayam has to be the natural choice | Understand customer needs; distinctive designs; comfort, value and practicality; a stronger value proposition |

## From forces to strategy

| Force | Strategic direction |
|---|---|
| Competitive rivalry | Brand differentiation |
| Threat of new entrants | Customer loyalty and brand recognition |
| Bargaining power of suppliers | Better sourcing strategy |
| Bargaining power of buyers | Customer experience and perceived value |
| Threat of substitutes | Stronger product positioning |

Together these point toward a **sustainable competitive advantage**: a position that is hard to copy and built over time.
