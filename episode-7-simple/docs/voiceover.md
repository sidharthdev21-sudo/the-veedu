## From research to reality  (0:00)

For six episodes, we researched the market. Now it's time to ask a simpler question: can Aalayam work as a real business?
To find out, we did four real things. We posted on Instagram. We opened an online store. We travelled across India to meet suppliers. And we planned a pop-up event in Kochi.

## The store today  (0:18)

Here's what the store looks like today.
Thirteen products are live. Most are cotton co-ord sets, priced between one thousand and seventeen hundred rupees.
The brand's promise is simple: comfortable clothes, in sizes L to triple XL, at an honest price.

## What's working  (0:34)

Two things are already working well.
First, honest prices. No product shows a fake original price with a big discount.
Second, help with size. Customers can send their measurements on WhatsApp, and the team tells them which size to buy.

## What to fix  (0:48)

And three things need fixing.
One: sizes. The promise is L to triple XL, but only two of the nine co-ord sets come in all four of those sizes.
Two: product pages. Only one of thirteen shows the garment's actual measurements, and fit is what shoppers worry about most.
Three: results weren't written down. The first Instagram campaign went well, but nobody recorded the numbers. Without numbers, you can't tell what really worked.

## A simple rule  (1:15)

So before adding any new product, Aalayam can ask three simple questions.
Do customers want it? Can a supplier make it in L to triple XL? And after all the costs, is there money left over?
Three yeses means: order a small batch and test it. Any no means: fix it first, or skip it.

## Next 90 days  (1:32)

The plan for the next ninety days has three steps.
First, write things down: every post, every enquiry, every supplier, and add measurements to every product.
Then, test: talk to customers, get samples from suppliers, and try new posts.
Finally, decide: launch a small batch, change the plan, or stop.
We moved from understanding the market to testing the business. Next, we turn what we learned into a business that works, again and again.
