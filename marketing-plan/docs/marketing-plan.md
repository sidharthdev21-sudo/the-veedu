## 4. Segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP)

### Segmentation (four lenses from the lesson)

| Lens | Segments | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic | Kerala · Malayali women in Indian metros (Bengaluru, Chennai, Mumbai) · NRI (later) | |
| Demographic | Women 25–40 · sizes L–3XL · ₹1,000–1,700 per outfit | |
| Behavioural | Online buyers who have returned clothes for fit · women who use a tailor · festival buyers (Onam) | |
| **Psychographic (most powerful)** | "Comfort-first, no-fuss professional" · "Has given up on online sizing" · "Wants Kerala roots in modern wear" | |

### Targeting: attractiveness × strength (GE-McKinsey logic)

| Segment | Attractiveness | Aalayam's strength | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. L–3XL women in Kerala who buy everyday co-ords online** | High: underserved, fit pain point, reachable on Instagram and WhatsApp | High: Kochi base, WhatsApp fit help, L–3XL focus | **Primary** |
| B. Malayali women in metros | Medium-high | Medium: same identity, online only | **Secondary** (from Day 60) |
| C. Festival buyers (Onam, Vishu) | Seasonal spikes | Medium: saree and set mundu at ₹850–900 | **Seasonal** |
| D. NRI | Attractive but costly logistics | Low today (owner: not live) | **Later**, waitlist only |
| E. All women, all sizes | Large but crowded | Low: no advantage | **Avoid** |

### Buyer persona: "Anu" (an illustrative persona built from research, not a real customer)

> 32, project coordinator in Kochi, wears XL top and 2XL bottom. Buys clothes on Instagram but has returned two co-ords this year because "the pants didn't fit". Values comfort, cotton and looking put-together for work. Scrolls Instagram late in the evening. Trusts real women's photos more than models. Will message a brand on WhatsApp before buying.

### Positioning statement

**For** women who wear L to 3XL and are tired of guessing sizes online, **Aalayam** is the Kochi-born clothing brand that makes **soft cotton co-ords that fit the first time**. **Unlike** discount-led brands with hundreds of styles, Aalayam shows real measurements, prices honestly and helps every customer choose her size on WhatsApp.

**Brand line (to test):** *Fits the first time.* Alternatives to test: *Comfort, in your size.* · *Honest fits.*

## 5. Consumer behaviour: how she decides

### AIDAS journey with content for each stage

| Stage | What "Anu" is thinking | Content / touchpoint | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | "Co-ord sets look comfy…" | Reels: real L–3XL women in motion; nano creators; Google Business Profile | Reach, 3-second views |
| **Interest** | "Would it fit me?" | Carousel: "How we measure" · size guide · fabric close-ups | Saves, profile visits |
| **Desire** | "I want this one, in my size" | Garment measurements on the page · try-on Stories · reviews | Link clicks, add-to-cart |
| **Action** | "Let me check my size first" | WhatsApp fit check → recommended size → checkout link | Conversion rate, fit-check → order rate |
| **Satisfaction** | "It actually fits!" | Thank-you note with care card · review request at Day 7 · "fit memory" for next order | Reviews, repeat rate, exchange rate |

**Rule from the lesson:** match the message to the stage she is actually in. Don't run "Buy now" ads to people who have never heard of Aalayam.

### Three drivers

| Driver | For Aalayam |
|---|---|
| **Motivation (Maslow)** | Safety: "will it fit? will it be see-through?" · Belonging: "clothes made for women like me" · Esteem: "I look put-together at work" |
| **Perception** | Built by real-size models, honest prices with no fake MRP, clean store design, reviews and WhatsApp responsiveness |
| **Memory** | Sensory: the soft cotton feel and a signature unboxing (a care card and tissue). Classical conditioning: the same "Fits the first time" line and look everywhere. Operant conditioning: "fit memory" saves her sizes; a thank-you reward on the second order. Cognitive: Q&A content for research ("Which co-ord fits 3XL?") |

### F-A-B for the hero co-ord

| Layer | Hero co-ord set |
|---|---|
| **Feature** | 60×60 cotton; garment measurements published; L–3XL; relaxed fit; pockets; WhatsApp size check |
| **Advantage** | Breathable in Kerala's heat; she knows the fit before buying; no exchange hassle |
| **Benefit (lead with this)** | *"Feel confident and comfortable all day, without worrying about what fits."* |

### How involved is the purchase? (ELM)

Clothing is medium involvement, but **fit makes it high-involvement for plus-size buyers**. So:
- **Rational route (main):** measurements, fabric facts, real-size photos, reviews.
- **Emotional route (supporting):** comfort, confidence, Kerala warmth.

**Hero test (Curatio method):** A/B test four creatives: *product only* · *model only* · *real L–3XL customer* · *customer + measurement overlay*. Let the data choose.

## 6. Brand: identity prism and equity

### Kapferer Brand Identity Prism

| Facet | Aalayam |
|---|---|
| **Physique** (what you see) | Soft cotton co-ords, earthy block prints, relaxed cuts; a dark-green or earthy palette; real women in L–3XL |
| **Personality** | Warm, honest, practical, quietly confident ("we'll tell you honestly which size", from the store's own size guide) |
| **Culture** (values) | Honesty (no fake MRP, no fake urgency), comfort over trends, Kerala roots |
| **Relationship** | A friend who knows clothes: personal WhatsApp help, "we'll say so if a style won't suit you" |
| **Reflection** (who buys it) | Grounded, working Malayali women who value ease and quality |
| **Self-image** (how she feels) | "I don't have to fight my clothes. I look good, and I'm comfortable." |

### Differentiation

- **Functional:** fit proof (measurements, L–3XL grading, WhatsApp fit check) and breathable cotton.
- **Symbolic:** an honest brand made for bigger bodies, rooted in Kerala.

**Brand equity drivers to build:** awareness (Reels, creators), perceived quality (fabric and fit proof), associations ("fits the first time", "honest"), loyalty (fit memory, second-order reward).

### Internal marketing (Service Marketing Triangle)

Everyone who touches the customer must live the brand: the WhatsApp replier, the packer, the supplier who grades sizes. A one-page **brand promise card** sets three rules: reply within a few hours, never push a size that won't fit, and always state measurements.

## 7. Growth direction: Ansoff

| Quadrant | Aalayam option | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Market penetration** | Sell more co-ords to L–3XL women in Kerala (better pages, reviews, WhatsApp) | **Now** |
| **Product development** | "Fits the First Time" capsule: full L–3XL, measurements, possibly separate top and bottom sizes (Episode 6) | **Now**, as a small capsule |
| **Market development** | Malayali women in metros; NRI later | **Next / later** |
| **Diversification** | Kidswear, home textiles | **Not now** |

**The Atomberg mixie lesson applies here:** a new product only works if its price premium is believable. Aalayam's saree (₹900) and set mundu (₹850) are priced as accessible festival items. Keep them seasonal, don't let them dilute the co-ord focus, and don't launch anything priced far above what the customer expects from the brand.

## 9. Search: SEO, SEM and GEO

### Keywords (to validate in Google Keyword Planner; volumes not yet checked)

| Journey stage | Type | Example keywords |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Short-tail | co ord set for women · cotton co ord set |
| Interest | Mid | plus size co ord set · co ord set 3XL · comfortable office co ord set |
| Decision | Long-tail | cotton co ord set for women XXL online India · co ord set Kochi · Kerala cotton co ord set online |
| Festival | Long-tail | set mundu online 850 · Onam saree online under 1000 |

### SEO (slow, free; built on EEAT)

1. **Experience and expertise:** product descriptions with real measurements, fabric (60×60 cotton) and care. A blog with "How to measure for a co-ord set".
2. **Authority:** backlinks from Instagram, Google Business Profile, marketplace listings and Kochi local media and blogs.
3. **Local SEO:** Google Business Profile with pop-up and event photos; ask happy Kochi customers for Google reviews.
4. **Answer box:** Q&A headings on the size guide: "What size co-ord should I buy if I'm between XL and XXL?"

### SEM (fast, paid): a small test only

- Run Google Search ads only on **long-tail, high-intent** terms ("cotton co ord set 3XL"), geo-targeted to Kerala, with a daily cap.
- Start once product pages have measurements; paid traffic to weak pages wastes money.
- Measure cost per click and cost per order; stop or scale after 4 weeks.

### GEO (AI search)

Publish structured Q&A that AI assistants can quote:
> **"Where can I buy cotton co-ord sets in 3XL in Kerala?"** Aalayam (Kochi) makes cotton co-ord sets in L to 3XL, priced ₹1,000–1,700, with garment measurements on each product and a WhatsApp size check.

Keep facts accurate and specific (sizes, prices, fabric), and cite the measurement method.

| **2. Market** | Primary: "Anu", L–3XL women 25–40 in Kerala. Secondary: Malayali women in metros (from week 9). Influencers: the people she trusts, meaning real women her size. |
| **3. Media** | **Instagram** Reels and Stories (main); **WhatsApp** (fit check, catalogue, broadcast); **Shopify** (all links land here, with UTMs); **Google** (Business Profile, SEO, small SEM); **Facebook** (optional, older relatives); **Kochi pop-up** (try-on, offline trust). Dominate two channels well rather than six badly (from the 7Ms notes). |
| **4. Messaging** | Deepest need (Maslow): *safety and confidence in fit*. High involvement (ELM), so lead with proof. **Pillars:** 1) Fit proof (measurements, real sizes); 2) Comfort (cotton, Kerala heat); 3) Honesty (no fake MRP, we'll tell you if it won't fit); 4) Kerala roots (Onam, Kochi). **A/B tests:** product-as-hero vs user-as-hero; "Fits the first time" vs "Comfort, in your size". |

| **6. Moment** | **Season:** Christmas and New Year (Dec), wedding season, Pongal and Sankranti (Jan), Valentine's (Feb); prepare **Vishu (April) and Onam** content early. **Time of day:** test evening slots (lesson insight: Curatio's mothers scrolled at ~10 PM). | **7. Metrics** | Awareness: reach, 3-second views, saves, profile visits. Trial: link clicks, fit checks started, add-to-cart, conversion, cost per order. Repeat: repeat rate, reviews, size-exchange rate. Always **matched to the mission**: don't judge an awareness Reel by sales. |
| **+ Management** | Founder owns strategy and WhatsApp; one freelancer or student for editing; weekly 30-minute review of the dashboard. |

### Content mix per week (organic)

| Format | Count | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | 3 | "Same set on L and on 3XL" · "How we measure every garment" · "Monsoon-proof cotton" |

Social reach is becoming less reliable, and people scroll more than they post. So every campaign also builds **owned audiences**: an opt-in WhatsApp broadcast list, an email list and Google reviews. Measure the share of orders that come from owned channels.

## 12. Launch and growth: from signalling to moat

### Four novelties Aalayam faces, and the signals that answer them

| Novelty | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Firm** (a new brand) | Real customer reviews with photos and sizes; Google Business Profile reviews; pop-up presence |
| **Management** (first-time founders) | Founder-story Reel; consistent, professional posting; a clear About page (already strong) |
| **Technology / product** (a new kind of fit promise) | Published measurements; "fit-tested on L–3XL bodies" content; transparent fabric facts |
| **Provenance** (small Kochi label) | Turn it into a strength: "Made for Kerala's weather, by a Kochi brand" |

### Pre-launch (T0 = capsule launch, about Day 45)

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| T-45 | Choose the 6 styles; confirm suppliers can make **all of L–3XL** (the supplier gate) |
| T-30 | Samples received; fit-tested on real L–3XL women; measurements recorded |
| T-21 | Product pages with measurements and real-size photos; creator shortlist |
| T-14 | Teaser Reels; WhatsApp waitlist opens |
| T-7 | Creator seeding; pop-up date fixed (one date, unlike the two dates on last year's posters) |

### Launch: **soft launch**

Pre-orders to the waitlist first, then the public. Watch a 60–90 day dashboard. **First impressions stick**, so get fit right for a small group before scaling.

### Growth hacks (low-cost entry points)

1. **WhatsApp fit check** as the main CTA ("Send your measurements, we'll tell you your size").
2. **Pop-up try-on days** in Kochi, with every visitor added to the WhatsApp list (opt-in).
3. **"Bring a friend your size"** referral: both get a thank-you on the next order.
4. **Festival bundles:** saree or set mundu + co-ord around Vishu and Onam.

### Growth loops (customers bring customers)

- **Review loop:** at Day 7, ask for a photo review with her size; reviews become Reels and Stories, which bring new customers, who leave more reviews.
- **Fit-memory loop:** saved sizes make reorders effortless; happy repeat buyers refer friends.

### Hyper-scaling (later)

Templates and quick replies for WhatsApp; a size-recommendation form on the store; a Shopify app for reviews. Growth then doesn't need a person per order.

### Durable moat

1. **Fit data:** measurements from WhatsApp fit checks (stored with consent) show which sizes and cuts Kerala's L–3XL women actually need. Competitors can't buy this.
2. **Community:** a loyal, size-inclusive customer base and its UGC.
3. **Trust:** a reputation for honesty (no fake MRP), which is hard to copy credibly.

## 13. Sustainability and IP

### Intellectual property

| Type | Action |
|---|---|
| **Trademark** | Check and register "Aalayam by Anamika" (name and logo) in the clothing class, so lookalike sellers can't copy it |
| **Copyright** | Original product photos, designs, Reels and copy are Aalayam's; watermark lookbook images |
| **Trade secrets** | Supplier list, costings and the fit dataset stay private (and never appear in videos) |
| **Patents** | Not applicable to garments. Unique prints can be protected via design registration (check with a lawyer) |

### Triple Bottom Line

| Pillar | Aalayam |
|---|---|
| People | Size inclusivity; fair pay for tailors and suppliers (ask and record) |
| Planet | Fewer returns means less shipping and waste; cotton; less packaging |
| Profit | Fewer exchanges protect margin; honest pricing builds loyalty |

### 6R applied at the design stage (75–80% of impact is decided here)

| R | Action |
|---|---|
| Reduce | Fewer size returns through measurements and the fit check; lighter, plastic-free packaging |
| Reuse | Cloth garment bag that doubles as a storage pouch |
| Recycle | Paper mailers; recycled tissue |
| Repair | Free button and hem fixes for 30 days |
| Refurbish | Lightly tried pop-up samples sold as "sample sale" items |
| Rethink | Separate top and bottom sizes (Episode 6): a fit fix that also cuts returns |

**Claims rule (the toothbrush lesson): measure before you claim.** Don't call anything "eco" or "sustainable" until it's verified. Say what's true: "cotton", "plastic-free packaging".

## 14. Budget method

**Method:** objective-based, ground-up budgeting (from the 7Ms notes). Work backwards from the order goal.

```
Paid sessions needed = paid orders goal ÷ conversion rate
Paid media budget    = paid sessions needed × cost per click
```

**Worked example: *illustrative assumption, not Aalayam data*.** Replace every input after Day 30.

| Input | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Paid orders goal per month | 30 | Illustrative |
| Conversion rate | 1% | Illustrative benchmark (the real rate comes from Shopify) |
| Paid sessions needed | 30 ÷ 0.01 = **3,000** | Calculated |
| Cost per click | ₹10 | Illustrative (the course example) |
| **Monthly paid media** | 3,000 × ₹10 = **₹30,000** | Calculated |
| Plus creators | 12 nano × ₹3,000 = ₹36,000 per quarter | Course tier range (₹1K–5K) |

**Check against margin:** at the store's median price of ₹1,200, contribution is about ₹367 per order with illustrative costs (Episode 7). So the cost per order must stay well below that. **If paid cost per order exceeds contribution, stop and fix conversion first.**

**Allocation by mission:** awareness 40% · trial 40% · repeat and retargeting 10% · test reserve 10%.

## 17. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Size coverage stays thin (2 of 9 co-ords in full L–3XL) | Supplier L–3XL gate; capsule only in full sizes |
| Paid spend before pages are ready | Fix measurements first; SEM only after week 2 |
| Influencer fraud or poor fit with creators | Flat fees, codes, anomaly checks; only creators who wear L–3XL |
| Posting Zero / falling organic reach | Build WhatsApp and email lists; Google reviews |
| Claims that can't be backed up ("30% discount", "international shipping", "eco") | Confirm or remove; measure before claiming |
| Small sample sizes mislead | Show n on every metric; decide over 4+ weeks |
| Founder time | Templates, quick replies, one freelancer for editing |
