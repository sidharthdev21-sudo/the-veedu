## Recap  (0:00)

In Episode 4, we listened to customers and sized the market. Shoppers praised comfort, struggled with fit, and doubted product photos. And we estimated a serviceable market of about two thousand three hundred crore rupees.
Now comes the harder part: deciding where to focus.

## The strategic question  (0:16)

Aalayam sells co-ord sets, sarees and kurta sets, through its website, Instagram, marketplaces and exhibitions.
A small brand can't invest equally in all of them. So the question is: where should Aalayam focus its resources?
We used three frameworks. SWOT diagnoses the brand's position. The BCG matrix checks market growth and share. And the GE-McKinsey matrix weighs market attractiveness against competitive strength.
One note: Aalayam's internal sales data isn't available yet, so this analysis is provisional, and every assumption is labelled.

## SWOT analysis  (0:50)

Start with SWOT.
Strengths: an accessible price, right at the twelve-hundred-rupee mid-price benchmark, a comfort-first promise, and Kerala roots, in the region that buys half of India's sarees.
Weaknesses: no public reviews yet, no category-level data, and a small team spread across three categories and four channels.
Opportunities: women's apparel is growing about twelve percent a year, online's share is rising to a quarter, and sets and separates are growing faster than sarees.
Threats: discounts of up to sixty-nine percent, five-hundred-rupee unbranded sets, expanding value retailers, and costly returns.

## From diagnosis to action  (1:27)

Next, TOWS turns that diagnosis into action, by pairing each side of the matrix.
Strengths with opportunities: make comfort co-ords with a Kerala design signature the hero line.
Weaknesses with opportunities: build social proof, and start measuring everything.
Strengths against threats: compete on transparency and one honest price, not discounts.
And weaknesses against threats: prune generic kurta sets, and stay out of the price war.

## BCG Growth-Share Matrix  (1:53)

Now the BCG matrix. It plots market growth against relative market share.
From Technopak's data, sets grow about eleven percent a year, separates about fifteen, and sarees about eight.
Aalayam's market share can't be measured yet, and followers or product counts aren't market share. As a young brand, its share is far below the leaders in every category.
So co-ords and kurta sets land as Question Marks, and sarees as a provisional Dog. There are no Stars or Cash Cows yet, which is normal for an early-stage brand.

## GE-McKinsey 9-Box Matrix  (2:25)

Because every share is low, BCG can't tell the categories apart very well. The GE-McKinsey matrix looks deeper.
We scored industry attractiveness on six weighted criteria, such as market size, growth, competition and regional fit.
And competitive strength on six more, such as differentiation, price competitiveness, channels and repeat potential.
Co-ords score highest on both, at three point six and three point four. Sarees follow, helped by the South Indian market. Kurta sets score lowest on strength.
All three sit in the middle band. No category has earned heavy investment yet. Aalayam's strength still has to be proven.

## Strategic synthesis  (3:03)

Now, put the three frameworks together.
They agree on two things: co-ord sets are the best bet, and generic kurta sets are the weakest place to invest.
They disagree on sarees. BCG sees slow growth. GE and SWOT see a regional asset. So sarees become a focused Kerala line, not a national push.

## Recommendation and 90-day plan  (3:22)

That leads to three priorities.
One: make comfort co-ords the hero category, with a Kerala design signature and full fit transparency.
Two: keep sarees as a focused Kerala line, sold through exhibitions and Instagram.
Three: build the evidence engine. Track sales, returns, repeat purchases and reviews, and prune kurta sets to proven bestsellers.
Over the next ninety days: measure in the first month, test a six-piece Kerala co-ord capsule and fit-transparent product pages in the second, and re-score every matrix with real data in the third.

## Closing  (3:55)

Strategy isn't doing everything. It's choosing where not to spend.
With evidence instead of guesses, Aalayam by Anamika can focus its limited resources where they'll build a stronger, more sustainable business.
