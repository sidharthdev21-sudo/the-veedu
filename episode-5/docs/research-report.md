## 4. BCG Growth–Share Matrix (provisional)

**Portfolio units:** co-ord sets · sarees · kurta sets and kurtis.

**Market growth axis:** category CAGR FY24–29P from Technopak. The high/low cut-off is **10%**, the classic BCG threshold; women's apparel overall grows 11.9%. Co-ord sets aren't separated in the report, so they're shown as a range: sets at 10.7% to mix & match at 15.5%.

**Relative market share axis:** RMS = Aalayam's market share ÷ the largest competitor's share. **Neither number is available [M].** Instagram followers, product counts and site traffic are *not* market share, so they aren't used. Given that Aalayam has no public listings and competitors operate at marketplace scale, **RMS is assumed to be far below 1.0 for every unit [H]**. That puts every unit on the low-share side.

| Unit | Market growth | RMS | Quadrant | Confidence | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Co-ord sets | 10.7–15.5% (high) | ≪ 1 (assumed) | **Question Mark** | Medium on growth, low on share | **Invest selectively**: back it as the hero, with tests |
| Kurta sets / kurtis | 10.7% sets, 15.5% kurtis (high) | ≪ 1 (assumed) | **Question Mark**, crowded | Low | **Test selectively**: keep only proven SKUs |
| Sarees | 8.3% (low) | ≪ 1 (assumed) | **Dog** (provisional) | Medium on growth, low on share | **Reconsider scope**: keep a focused regional line, don't scale nationally |

**Reading:** no Stars or Cash Cows. That's expected for an early-stage brand, because a Cash Cow needs a leading share. BCG mainly tells Aalayam that it can't fund three Question Marks equally.

### Placement

| Unit | Attractiveness | Strength | Box | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Co-ord sets | 3.60 · Medium (top of the band) | 3.40 · Medium (top of the band) | Centre, nearest "Invest/Grow" | **Selectively invest, building toward grow** |
| Sarees | 3.40 · Medium | 3.15 · Medium | Centre | **Selectively invest: focused and regional** |
| Kurta sets | 3.25 · Medium | 2.75 · Medium (low side) | Centre, nearest "Harvest" | **Manage for earnings; prune** |

**Why GE–McKinsey is more nuanced than BCG:** it weighs many factors, not just growth and share. It separates *how attractive a market is* from *how strong Aalayam is in it*, and it uses a 3×3 grid instead of four boxes. For a small brand, BCG's share axis is low for every unit, so it can't tell the units apart. GE scoring can.

All three units sitting in the central band is itself a finding: **no category has yet earned an "invest heavily" position.** Aalayam's competitive strength has to be built and proven.

## 6. Strategic synthesis

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What SWOT shows | Strengths in positioning (comfort, price, Kerala) but weaknesses in proof (no reviews, no data) and focus (thinly spread) |
| What BCG shows | Every unit is low-share; co-ords and kurta sets are Question Marks, sarees a provisional Dog |
| What GE–McKinsey shows | Co-ords score highest on both axes; kurta sets lowest on strength; sarees are mid-range thanks to the South India market and accessible price |
| **Where they agree** | **Co-ord sets are the best bet; kurta sets are the weakest place to invest** |
| **Where they differ** | **Sarees:** BCG says Dog (slow growth); GE and SWOT say it's a regional asset. Resolution: keep sarees as a focused Kerala line, don't scale nationally |
| What to prioritise | Hero co-ords, a focused regional saree line, and an evidence engine before scaling |

## 7. Top three strategic priorities

1. **Make comfort co-ords the hero category.** Build them around a Kerala design signature, sell online-first through Shopify and Instagram, and make comfort and fit provable with measurements, fit notes and true-colour photos.
2. **Keep sarees as a focused Kerala line.** Sell them through exhibitions and Instagram, priced as accessible everyday sarees. No national saree push until data supports it.
3. **Build the evidence engine before scaling.** Track category and channel sales, returns with reasons, repeat purchases and reviews. Prune kurta sets to proven bestsellers.

**Areas to validate:** sales and margin by category · return reasons · repeat rate · customer perception survey (Episode 3 axes) · NRI demand · whether co-ords sit in Technopak's "sets" or "mix & match".

**Risks:** the co-ord trend could plateau · discount pressure · capacity of a small team · these provisional scores could be wrong.

**Experiments**

| # | Experiment | Measure | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Fit-transparency product pages (measurements + fit notes) on 10 co-ords vs the current pages | Conversion, size-related returns | Higher conversion, fewer size returns |
| E2 | A 6-piece Kerala-design co-ord capsule, launched with Instagram pre-orders | Pre-orders, 30-day sell-through | Sell-through above the current average |
| E3 | Sarees at one Kerala exhibition vs Instagram | Cost per sale, average order value | Lower cost per sale offline |
| E4 | NRI waitlist landing page | Sign-ups by country | A threshold of sign-ups before shipping |
| E5 | Prune kurta-set SKUs to the top 20% | Inventory turns, margin | Same revenue on fewer SKUs |
