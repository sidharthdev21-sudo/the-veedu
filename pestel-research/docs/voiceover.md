# Voiceover script

Voice: Kokoro (af_heart, US English), speed 1.15. Total length 3:56.

## Introduction  (0:00)

This is Aalayam by Anamika, an Indian brand making comfortable co-ords and sarees for women.
Like every business, it grows inside a bigger world: tax, prices, trends, technology, the planet and the law.
We wanted to understand that world properly, so we ran a PESTEL analysis.
PESTEL looks at six outside forces: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental and Legal. Here's how we did it.

## How we approached the research  (0:25)

We worked in four steps.
First, we got to know the business: what Aalayam sells, who it sells to, and where it fits in Indian women's fashion.
Second, we split PESTEL into its six parts and wrote questions for a clothing brand, like: how does GST affect the price of an outfit?
Third, we collected information: government publications, industry reports, fashion trends and news on online shopping.
Fourth, we analysed it, asking of each finding: is this an opportunity or a threat, and what does it mean for Aalayam?

## Our research toolkit  (0:55)

Here's the toolkit we used.
Google Search helped us find reports and trends, and government websites gave reliable facts on GST, textile policy and consumer rules.
Google Trends showed which styles people were searching for, and industry reports described the wider apparel market.
Claude helped us organise our findings and spot patterns. ChatGPT helped us brainstorm questions and draft recommendations.
Google Sheets kept every finding sorted under its PESTEL heading, and Canva helped us present the result.
AI tools sped up the work, but we checked the facts against the original sources. Next, we'd recommend customer interviews and Aalayam's own sales data.

## Political  (1:35)

Political. We looked at textile policy, Make in India, GST and trade rules.
The government actively supports Indian textile manufacturing, and the GST on a garment depends on its price.
That shapes where Aalayam sources fabric, and how it prices each collection.

## Economic  (1:52)

Economic. We looked at inflation, incomes, fabric costs and competition.
Shoppers are price-conscious, fabric costs can change quickly, and online competition is strong.
So pricing has to be clear, fair and carefully planned.

## Social  (2:06)

Social. We looked at what women want to wear, and how they shop.
Comfort is a priority from workdays to weekends, and social media drives how new styles are discovered.
That suits a brand built on comfortable everyday fashion.

## Technological  (2:19)

Technological. We looked at online stores, AI recommendations, digital marketing and analytics.
Small brands can now reach customers across India by phone, and data shows what people actually like.
That's a real chance for Aalayam to grow online.

## Environmental  (2:34)

Environmental. We looked at sustainable fabrics, textile waste and packaging.
More shoppers care how their clothes are made, and India has banned many single-use plastics.
Responsible choices can build trust.

## Legal  (2:47)

Legal. We looked at consumer protection, e-commerce rules, labelling, GST compliance and data privacy.
Online sellers must show clear prices and return policies, label products properly, and protect customer data.
Getting this right keeps the business safe and trusted.

## From research to business insights  (3:03)

Next, we turned the findings into ideas: recommendations to consider, not things Aalayam already does.
Political: understand tax and textile policy before pricing and choosing suppliers. Economic: try affordable collections and outfit bundles.
Social: make comfortable everyday fashion the heart of the brand. Technological: explore AI-assisted marketing and personalised recommendations.
Environmental: consider sustainable packaging and responsible sourcing. Legal: make sure listings, prices, labels and online processes follow the rules.

## Conclusion  (3:37)

So that was our journey: understand the business, ask questions, gather information, analyse it with AI's help, sort it into PESTEL, and turn it into insights.
PESTEL doesn't predict the future, but it helps Aalayam by Anamika see its world clearly and make better-informed decisions.
