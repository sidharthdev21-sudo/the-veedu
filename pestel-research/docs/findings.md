# PESTEL findings and business implications: Aalayam by Anamika

How to read this: each factor lists what we investigated, what we found and why it matters to Aalayam, followed by a **potential recommendation**. The recommendations are ideas to consider. They are **not** capabilities Aalayam has today.

The findings describe general conditions for Indian women's fashion brands. They are not data about Aalayam's own sales, prices, customers or operations.

| Factor | What we investigated | What we discovered | Why it matters to Aalayam | Potential recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Political** | Textile policy, Make in India, GST, import and export rules | The government actively supports Indian textile manufacturing. GST on clothing depends on the item's price. | Shapes where Aalayam sources fabric and how it prices each collection. | Understand tax and textile policy before pricing and choosing suppliers. |
| **Economic** | Inflation, disposable income, purchasing power, fabric costs, competition | Shoppers are price-conscious, fabric costs can change quickly, and online competition is strong. | Pricing must be clear, fair and carefully planned. | Try affordable collections and outfit bundles. |
| **Social** | Fashion preferences, comfort-led clothing, lifestyle changes, social media, online shopping | Comfort comes first, from workdays to weekends. Social media drives how new styles are discovered. | Fits a brand built on comfortable everyday fashion. | Make comfortable everyday fashion the heart of the brand. |
| **Technological** | E-commerce platforms, AI recommendations, digital marketing, social commerce, customer analytics | Small brands can sell across India by phone, and data shows what customers like. | A real chance for Aalayam to grow online. | Explore AI-assisted marketing and personalised recommendations. |
| **Environmental** | Sustainable fabrics, textile waste, packaging, sustainability awareness | Shoppers increasingly care how clothes are made. India has banned many single-use plastic items. | Responsible choices build trust. | Consider sustainable packaging and responsible sourcing. |
| **Legal** | Consumer protection, e-commerce rules, product labelling, GST compliance, data privacy | Online sellers must show clear prices and return policies, label products properly, file GST correctly and protect customer data. | Keeps the business safe and trusted. | Make listings, prices, labels and online processes follow the rules. |

## Sources the factual points rest on

| Point | Basis |
|---|---|
| Support for Indian textile manufacturing | Make in India; textile schemes such as PM MITRA parks and the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme for textiles |
| GST on clothing depends on price | GST on apparel is charged at different rates depending on the sale value per piece |
| Single-use plastic ban | Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2021, ban in force from 1 July 2022 |
| Clear prices and return policies | Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 |
| Product labelling | Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 |
| Customer data protection | Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 |

Check current rates and rules on the official government sites before acting on them: rates and rules change.
