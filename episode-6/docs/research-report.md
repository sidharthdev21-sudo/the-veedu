### Common industry practices (what everyone does)

1. **Fixed-size sets:** a co-ord is sold as one size for both top and bottom [P].
2. **High MRP plus permanent discounts** [P].
3. **Wide, fast-changing ranges** (hundreds of styles) [P].
4. **Body-measurement size charts** rather than measurements of the garment itself; fit notes are rare [P, partial].
5. **Fit flexibility exists only at premium prices** (custom tailoring, slow and often non-returnable) [P].

### Primary growth direction: **product development for the existing customer**

- **Why not penetration alone?** MP1 and MP2 are cheap and score as high, but any competitor can add a size chart or a review request. On their own they make Aalayam better at selling *the same co-ord everyone sells*, which leads back to price. They become the **enablers** of the primary strategy rather than the strategy itself.
- **Why not market development first?** New markets would multiply an unsolved fit and returns problem, especially NRI orders, where an exchange crosses borders. Market development is the **next** step (MD1), once fit is proven.
- **Why not diversification?** It contradicts Episode 5's focus and has no evidenced customer need.
- **Why product development?** It sells to the customer Aalayam already reaches, through channels it already has, and turns the market's top pain point, fit, into the product.

## 5. Blue Ocean Strategy

### Competitive landscape: where brands look alike

- Competitors in the ₹1,000–2,000 band compete on **price, discount depth and range breadth** [P].
- They share the same **industry practices**: fixed-size sets, high MRP with constant discounts, and generic body size charts [P].
- **Fit flexibility** is offered only at the **premium end**, slowly, and often without returns [P].
- Customers' **main frustration**, fit, is left for them to solve: order two sizes and return one, or pay a tailor [C] [H].

### Strategy canvas

**Scoring method:** 1 = very low or absent, 5 = very high. Scores are **research-based estimates [H]** from listings, policies and review themes in Episodes 3–4 and this episode's searches. They describe competitor *groups*, not individual brands, and were not rated by customers. A customer survey (Days 1–30) should replace them.

| Factor (what customers weigh) | Unbranded sellers | Mass D2C brands | Premium / designer | **Aalayam (proposed)** | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price level | 1 | 3 | 5 | **3** | Observed prices [P] |
| Reliance on discounts | 2 | 5 | 2 | **1** | "Off MRP" badges [P]; premium partly estimated |
| Range breadth | 4 | 5 | 3 | **2** | Product counts [P] |
| Fit information | 1 | 3 | 3 | **5** | Size charts observed [P] |
| Fit flexibility | 1 | 1 | 4 | **5** | Fixed sets vs custom tailoring [P] |
| Fabric comfort | 2 | 3 | 4 | **4** | Review themes [C]; Aalayam's is a goal |
| Regional design identity | 1 | 2 | 4 | **4** | Brand positioning [P]; Aalayam's is a goal |
| Personal fit help | 1 | 1 | 3 | **5** | Service channels [P] |

**What the canvas shows:** unbranded sellers and mass D2C brands follow **the same curve shape**: high on discounts and range, low on fit information and flexibility. Premium brands solve fit, but at two to three times the price. **Aalayam's proposed curve breaks away**: it lowers discounts and range, and raises fit information, fit flexibility and personal help at a mid price. This is the gap. Aalayam's scores are *targets for the proposed offer*, not current performance.

### Potential blue ocean concepts

None of these is a proven blue ocean; all are **potential** opportunities that need validation.

| | **Concept 1: Fit-confident comfort co-ords** | **Concept 2: Kerala everyday** | **Concept 3: For "tailor-first" women** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer problem | Online co-ords don't fit; top and bottom may need different sizes | Modern everyday wear is generic; Kerala identity is kept for festive wear | Some women avoid ready-made online clothing and buy fabric for a tailor because ready-made sizes don't fit (noncustomers) |
| Target customer | Women 24–38 buying everyday co-ords online, between sizes or with different top and bottom sizes | Malayali women 22–40 in Kerala and metros | Women who currently use tailors instead of buying ready-made |
| Current alternatives | Order two sizes and return one; a local tailor; premium custom fit at ₹2,500+ | Generic national prints; festive kasavu sets | Fabric plus a tailor (slower, but fits) |
| Value innovation | Split sizing, adjustable waist, two lengths, garment measurements, a WhatsApp fit check, one free size exchange, at ₹1,000–1,700 | A recognisable Kerala detail on breathable cotton, designed for daily wear in a humid climate | Ready-made convenience with near-tailored fit, by combining Concept 1's features |
| Why differentiated | Fit flexibility is currently a premium feature [P] | No clear competitor found in our limited search [P] | Converts noncustomers instead of fighting for existing ones |
| Hard to copy because | Mass brands' fixed-set inventory model and hundreds of styles make split sizing costly to retrofit; founder-led fit help doesn't scale for them. **But** it is copyable in time; the advantage is focus and speed | Authentic regional roots and a local network | Depends on Concept 1 working |
| Evidence | Fit is the top complaint [C]; size drives returns [P]; custom fit only at premium [P] | Weak: limited search [P]; South India saree share [F] | Hypothesis only [H] |
| Test before investing | Share of customers whose top and bottom sizes differ; conversion and exchange rates; inventory balance | Pre-order demand for a Kerala-detail capsule | Interviews with women who use tailors |

**Selected concept: Concept 1, fit-confident comfort co-ords**, with Concept 2's Kerala detail as the design layer. It has the strongest evidence, solves the most common pain point, and keeps the price band. Concept 3 becomes a target segment for later research.

## 6. ERRC Grid (applied to Concept 1)

| Action | What changes | Why it matters | Customer need | Expected impact | Investment / capability | Risk or trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Eliminate** · inflated MRP and constant "% off" | One honest price shown, no fake MRP | Discount badges erode trust and margin (T1) | Value clarity | Protects margin; builds trust [H] | Pricing discipline | Fewer clicks on marketplaces where discount badges drive ranking |
| **Eliminate** · the "one size for the whole set" rule | Top and bottom bought in separate sizes at the set price | Fixed sets force a bad fit on one piece | Fit | Fewer size returns [H] | Separate stock for tops and bottoms; Shopify options | Inventory imbalance (more tops left than bottoms, or the reverse) |
| **Reduce** · range breadth | 6–8 core silhouettes per season in more colours, instead of hundreds of styles | Small team; fewer SKUs make split sizing manageable | Easier choice | Lower inventory risk; deeper stock per style [H] | Range planning | Less novelty for trend-led shoppers |
| **Reduce** · marketplace dependence | Marketplaces only for proven bestsellers; the website is the home of the fit offer | Fit help and split sizing need Aalayam's own channel | Trust, service | Better margin per order [H] | Shopify focus | Less reach in the short term |
| **Raise** · fit information | Garment measurements (not just a body chart), model height and size worn, fit notes, fabric opacity and lining, daylight photos | Fit and "looks different online" are the top doubts | Confidence before buying | Higher conversion; fewer returns [H] | Measuring and photography time | Time per product |
| **Raise** · exchange ease | One free size exchange per order | Removes the risk of ordering | Confidence | Higher conversion [H] | Reverse-logistics cost | Cost if exchange rates are high: track closely |
| **Raise** · Kerala design identity | One recognisable Kerala detail across the hero line | Makes the brand memorable and harder to copy | Identity | Recognition, word of mouth [H] | Design | Could read as festive if overdone |
| **Create** · split sizing with adjustable fit | Separate top and bottom sizes, an adjustable waist, two bottom lengths (petite and regular) | Fit flexibility currently costs ₹2,500+ elsewhere | Fit | The core differentiator [H] | Patterns, grading, stock | Pattern and production complexity |
| **Create** · a WhatsApp fit check | Customer shares three measurements; the team recommends sizes before dispatch; sizes are saved for reorders ("fit memory") | Personal help that mass brands don't offer | Fit, service | Fewer returns; repeat orders [H] | Team time on WhatsApp | Doesn't scale beyond a certain order volume without tools |

## 8. Final recommendation

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Primary growth direction** | Product development for the existing market |
| **Target customer** | Women aged 24–38 in Kerala, and Malayali women in Indian metros, who buy everyday co-ords online at ₹1,000–1,700 and have struggled with fit (for example, between sizes or with different top and bottom sizes) [H persona] |
| **Value proposition** | Comfort co-ords that fit the first time: choose your top and bottom sizes separately, with a Kerala touch, at one honest price |
| **Strategic concept** | Fit-confident comfort co-ords (a potential blue ocean, not yet validated) |

**Three immediate priorities**

1. **Validate the fit problem:** interviews and a survey on fit history, top and bottom sizes and tailor use, plus a Shopify baseline.
2. **Launch a 6-piece fit-confident capsule on pre-order**, with split sizing, garment measurements, an adjustable waist and the Kerala detail.
3. **Make fit visible and supported:** fit-transparent pages, the WhatsApp fit check, one free size exchange and review requests.

## 9. 90-day growth roadmap

| Phase | Actions | Output | KPIs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–30: Validate** | 15–20 customer interviews and a short survey (fit history, top and bottom sizes, tailor use, willingness to pay) · audit five competitors' size charts, exchange rules and prices · measure the top 10 current co-ords · record the Shopify baseline (conversion, return rate and reasons, repeat rate) · get split-sizing costs from the production partner | Validated (or rejected) pain point; a baseline; an operational plan | % of respondents with different top and bottom sizes; baseline conversion and size-return rate |
| **Days 31–60: Experiment** | Launch the 6-piece capsule on pre-order with split sizing · fit-transparent pages on the capsule and top 10 co-ords · WhatsApp fit check offered on every capsule order · one honest price, with no discount code · collect feedback 7 days after delivery | Experiment results and feedback | Pre-orders; conversion vs baseline; % of orders using split sizing; fit-check uptake; review count and rating |
| **Days 61–90: Decide and scale** | Compare results with the baseline · list what worked and what failed · decide: **scale** (extend split sizing to all co-ords), **iterate** (change the offer) or **stop** · set the next milestone: market development to Malayali women in metros (MD1) | A go / iterate / stop decision; next-quarter targets | Size-related exchange rate; exchange cost per order; sell-through; 60-day repeat rate |

**Proposed decision rules (to be set against the baseline from Days 1–30, not against invented numbers):**
- **Scale** if conversion rises and size-related returns fall compared with Aalayam's own baseline, *and* exchange cost per order stays within the margin the owner sets.
- **Iterate** if demand is there but inventory or exchange cost is a problem.
- **Stop** split sizing if few customers use it; keep the fit-information improvements, which are cheap.

No baselines or targets are given as numbers, because Aalayam's data is not yet available. All targets are **proposed**, not actual performance.
